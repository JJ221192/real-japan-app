What does your omikuji fortune say?

おみくじの楽しみ方

4 min readLast updated: 2026-10-12Cultural review: pending (sample content)

Overview

  • Omikuji are paper fortunes you draw at shrines and temples, usually for a small offering.
  • They rank your luck — from 大吉 (great blessing) to 凶 (bad luck) — and give advice on topics like health, travel and love.
  • Many visitors read them for fun as well as for meaning.

What travelers should know

  • Look for a box or machine marked おみくじ; some shrines offer English versions.
  • If the result is bad, many people tie it to the rack provided at the shrine to leave the bad luck behind.
  • Good fortunes are often kept in a wallet, but tying them is fine too.

How to do it

  1. 1Make an offering in the box.
  2. 2Shake the container or pick a slip.
  3. 3Read it, then keep it or tie it at the rack.

Exceptions & regional differences

Ranks and customs differ by shrine. Some only offer a few ranks, and some ask you not to tie slips on trees — use the rack.
