Wish plaques and shrine stamps

絵馬と御朱印

5 min readLast updated: 2026-10-12Cultural review: pending (sample content)

Overview

  • Ema are small wooden plaques where people write a wish and hang it at the shrine.
  • Goshuin are hand-written seals with the shrine's name and the date, collected in a special book.
  • A goshuin is a record of your visit to pray, not a souvenir stamp.

What travelers should know

  • Ema and goshuin are offered at the shrine office (社務所) for a small fee.
  • Pray first, then ask for a goshuin.
  • Write your wish in any language; avoid writing other people's personal information.

How to do it

  1. 1Ask with 御朱印をお願いします (goshuin o onegaishimasu) and hand over your book.

Exceptions & regional differences

Not every shrine offers goshuin, hours vary, and some give pre-written paper versions when busy.

Japanese you'll hear

  • お参りomairi · Visiting a shrine or temple to pray
  • ご利益goriyaku · The blessing or benefit a shrine is known for
